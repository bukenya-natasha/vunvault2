Module Module One

The Anatomy of a Social Engineering Attack

Unlike traditional hacking, social engineering doesn't exploit software, it exploits trust, urgency and fear. Understanding the pattern is the first step to resisting it.

Introduction to Phishing and Social Engineering
0:00 / 2:30

Cybercriminals follow a predictable lifecycle. First, information gathering, researching your name, job title, phone number and trusted contacts from social media or leaked data. Second, pretexting, inventing a believable scenario, like posing as a bank investigator, a supplier, or a relative in trouble. Third, the hook, contacting you with urgency or authority, demanding immediate action: click a link, send money, share a code. Finally, the exit, once you comply, they vanish, deleting messages or hanging up before you realise what happened.

The Golden Rule of Defense

Legitimate institutions will never rush you, threaten you, or ask for your full passwords, PINs, or one time passwords (OTPs). Urgency is the primary weapon of a fraudster, when you feel rushed, that feeling is itself the warning sign.

Module Module Two

Spotting Email & General Phishing

Email remains the single most common vector for fraud, and most phishing emails share the same tells once you know what to look for.

Red Flags in Email

Mismatched Sender

The display name says "PayPal Security" but the real address is something like [email protected]. Always check the actual address.

Generic Greeting

"Dear Customer" instead of your real name, legitimate services already know who you are.

Urgent Language

"Your account will be suspended in 24 hours" is designed to make you act before you think.

Suspicious Links

Hover over a link (don't click) to see its true destination before deciding.

Defensive Action, "Stop and Verify"

If an email claims to be from your bank or a colleague asking for money or data, don't use the links or numbers inside it. Open a new browser tab, type the official site address yourself, log in, and check for alerts there instead.

Module Module Three

Financial Platform Fraud, Mobile Money & PayPal

Mobile money and digital payment platforms are prime targets across Africa because funds move instantly and reversals are rare.

Mobile Money Fraud Tactics

Scenario, The "Wrong Number" Reversal Scam

You receive a fake mobile money message claiming money was sent to you. Moments later, a caller begs you to reverse the "accidental" transfer. The original SMS was spoofed, if you reverse it, you send your own money to the fraudster.

Defense: Never reverse money based on a phone call. Tell the caller to contact your provider's customer care to initiate a reversal themselves.

Scenario, The "Promotion / Upgrade" Scam

A caller poses as your mobile network operator, offering a loan-limit increase, and asks for your mobile money PIN or OTP to "process" it.

Defense: Legitimate providers will never ask for your PIN or OTP over the phone. Hang up immediately.

PayPal Fraud Tactics

Scenario, The Fake Invoice

You receive an email that looks like a PayPal invoice for something you never bought, with a "Dispute Charge" button leading to a fake login page that steals your credentials.

Defense: Log directly into your PayPal account through the app or a manually typed address. If no transaction is listed, the invoice is fake.

Module Module Four

The SIM Swap Threat

A SIM swap bypasses your phone's physical security entirely, the attacker becomes your phone number.

The fraudster gathers your personal information, impersonates you to your mobile provider, and transfers your number onto a SIM card they control. Your phone loses signal; the attacker now receives all your calls and SMS, including the 2FA codes protecting your bank and email.

Signs You May Be Under Attack

Your phone suddenly shows "No Service" or "SOS Only" in a normally strong-signal area, or you receive password-change or login notifications you never initiated.

Defensive Actions

1
Set a SIM PIN

Contact your provider and require a unique PIN for any account or SIM changes.

2
Act fast

If your phone loses signal unexpectedly, contact your provider immediately from another phone.

3
Use app-based 2FA

Authenticator apps are tied to your device, not your phone number, and are immune to SIM swaps.

Quick Check, Module Four

Module Module Five

Malware Delivered Through Social Engineering

Social engineering is often just the delivery mechanism, the real payload is malware that lands the moment you click.

  • Drive-by downloads, visiting a compromised website automatically downloads malicious code to your device.
  • Malicious macros, an Office file asks you to "Enable Macros" to view content; once enabled, a script installs malware.
Defense Mechanisms

Never enable macros unless you're certain the document is from a trusted internal source. Keep antivirus/endpoint protection active and updated. Enable automatic software updates, most malware exploits known, already-patched holes. Use an ad-blocker to reduce accidental clicks on malicious ads.

Module Module Six

Organisational Defense & Incident Reporting

For organisations, technical safeguards alone aren't enough, a culture of security, and a clear response plan, are what actually stop a small incident from becoming a major breach.

Building a Security Culture

Zero Trust mindset: "Verify, never trust." Employees should question unusual requests, even ones that appear to come from the CEO (Business Email Compromise). Clear verification protocols: an urgent wire-transfer request by email should always require a secondary check, like a call to the executive's known number. No-blame reporting: encourage staff to report suspicious emails or mistakes without fear of discipline; fear of blame is what turns a small incident into a major one.

If You Suspect a Scam, Act in This Order

1
Do not engage

Don't reply, stay on the call, or click any links.

2
Disconnect

If malware is suspected, disconnect the device from the internet immediately (Wi-Fi off, cable unplugged).

3
Change passwords

From a different, safe device, change passwords for email, banking and mobile money.

4
Report

Notify your IT department, your bank's fraud line, or your national cybercrime reporting channel.
