Interpol Report Highlights Rise in Artificial Intelligence Enabled Cybercrime Across Africa

Published 03 Aug 2026 Author VUNVAULT Editorial

Interpol warns that artificial intelligence is automating cyberattacks across the continent, with financial losses doubling over the past two years.

Artificial intelligence is now actively enabling and automating fifty five percent of all reported cybercrimes across Africa, making digital attacks faster, highly scalable, and exceptionally difficult for security platforms and law enforcement to detect. This critical finding headlines the newly released African Cyberthreat Assessment Report published by global police organization Interpol.

The comprehensive forty page report, compiled using operational intelligence and detailed survey data from thirty six African member countries, warns that digital criminality on the continent has evolved from isolated, amateur operations into a highly industrialized, borderless ecosystem. This rapid automation has driven a devastating surge in financial losses, which more than doubled over the past two years to reach four hundred and eighty four million US dollars, up from one hundred and ninety two million since twenty twenty four.

Mapping Regional Threat Vectors across the Continent

The Interpol assessment outlines a defining geographical split in how cybercriminals deploy these advanced technologies across different African subregions:

  • Eastern Africa: This region has emerged as a major hub for mobile money fraud, SIM swap conspiracies, and infrastructure targeted ransomware, where attackers exploit rapid digitization to paralyze local services and demand massive payments.
  • Central and Western Africa: Attackers here remain heavily focused on Business Email Compromise schemes and romance scams, increasingly leveraging generative artificial intelligence to write highly convincing, grammatically flawless email correspondence that targets corporate victims in Europe and North America.
  • Southern Africa: Boasting ultra high connectivity and advanced digital infrastructure, Southern Africa has become a prime magnet for global threat actors seeking to launch large scale, highly disruptive attacks against utility grids, ports, and financial institutions.

The report also highlights the disturbing rise of organized scam centers, with seventy two percent of surveyed nations confirming the active presence of physical, coordinates operations inside their borders, with the highest concentration located in Western and Southern Africa.

The Threat of Synthetic Media and AI Facilitated Extortion

In addition to corporate fraud, the rise of artificial intelligence has dramatically amplified social threats targeting individuals. Digital sextortion and online harassment, heavily facilitated by deepfake audio cloning and synthetic media, have become pervasive across the continent.

Interpol partners at TrendAI recorded approximately six hundred thousand digital sextortion detections over the past year alone, showcasing how easily cybercriminals can weaponize simple social media photos or voicemail greetings to craft convincing, highly damaging extortion campaigns. Furthermore, because local cybersecurity legislation remains highly fragmented and artificial intelligence readiness among law enforcement is alarmingly low, many of these organized networks operate with absolute impunity across regional borders.

Successful Counter Operations and the Path to Collective Defense

Despite the sobering statistics, Interpol emphasized that coordinated regional collaboration is delivering highly successful outcomes. Neal Jetton, Director of Interpol's Cybercrime unit, highlighted four massive joint operations, including Operation Serengeti, Operation Contender, Operation Sentinel, and Operation Red Card, which collectively resulted in more than one thousand five hundred arrests, the physical seizure of hundreds of compromised devices, and the direct recovery of over one hundred million US dollars in stolen assets.

Additionally, seventeen African nations enacted or amended critical cybercrime legislation over the past year, and countries like Senegal successfully launched advanced online reporting platforms to track violations. Interpol urges African states to continue prioritizing harmonized legal frameworks, establishing active, inter agency data sharing protocols between telecom operators, banks, and police forces, and investing heavily in local technical talent to close the critical digital defense gap.
