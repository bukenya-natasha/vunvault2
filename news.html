FBI, CISA and Allies Issue Joint Advisory on Emerging Gunra Ransomware

Six agencies including the FBI, CISA, NSA and South Korea’s National Police Agency released a joint #StopRansomware advisory detailing Gunra’s double-extortion playbook.

By VUNVAULT News Desk 10 Aug 2026 ~7 min read

Six agencies just put a name on the ransomware strain that has been quietly industrialising since last spring. On 10 August 2026, CISA and the FBI, working with the NSA, South Korea’s National Police Agency and other partners, published #StopRansomware: Gunra — advisory AA26-222A — with tactics, mitigations and a STIX package of indicators.

Gunra first appeared in April 2025, built on Conti source code that leaked in 2022. By early 2026 it was a formal ransomware-as-a-service programme: a panel, a builder, Windows and Linux lockers, affiliate docs, and a recruitment drive aimed at penetration testers and “ethical hackers” who will sell initial access for a cut. One public pitch promised affiliates 80% of the ransom. Branding aliases include Golden Community.

How they walk in

There is nothing mystical about the front door. The FBI says actors exploit known holes in internet-facing firewalls and SSL-VPNs, especially Fortinet FortiOS and FortiProxy flaws CVE-2024-55591 (critical authentication bypass) and CVE-2025-24472. Korean investigators also watched them abuse exposed SSH, steal session cookies off VPN appliances, and impersonate staff so MFA never fires.

Most of the noisy work happens between 10 p.m. and 6 a.m. local time. They steal first — contracts, databases, mail, personal data — then encrypt. Negotiation happens on Tor. The leak-site clock is five to seven days. In at least one case they deleted backups in both the primary data centre and the disaster-recovery site, which is how “we have backups” becomes a press statement instead of a recovery plan.

Windows versus Linux

The Windows encryptor stamps files .ENCRT and uses a cryptographically sound generator; do not pay on the hope of a trick. The later Linux variant stamps .GNRA. Agencies say some Linux victims can recover files if original timestamps were preserved and nobody has already rebooted or “cleaned up.” If you have already touched the disk, call incident response before you decide the data is gone.

The group has encrypted as much as 9 terabytes in a single window using ChaCha20 and Salsa20, chosen for speed. Hospitals, governments and industrial operators are on the leak site. Industrial ransomware incidents hit a documented 1,140 in a single recent quarter. Chris Butera, CISA’s acting executive assistant director for cybersecurity, called Gunra “another variant in the ongoing trend” — which is bureaucrat for: this will keep happening.

What to do before Friday

  • Patch FortiOS / FortiProxy and every other VPN or firewall on the KEV list. If you cannot patch today, disable the HTTP/HTTPS admin interface or lock it to jump-host IPs.
  • Turn off default admin accounts. Audit domain admins. Require phishing-resistant MFA on every remote path.
  • Keep offline, immutable backups in a network you cannot reach from the production domain. Test a restore.
  • Segment so a single compromised laptop cannot see the backup VLAN, the OT network or every file server.
  • Watch off-hours. Gunra prefers the night shift.

African hospitals, SACCOs and ministries run the same Fortinet boxes and the same “we will patch next change window” culture. Gunra does not need a local affiliate to hurt you if the VPN is still on last year’s firmware. Read the advisory. Then close the door it describes.

Key facts at a glance

  • Joint advisory AA26-222A, published 10 August 2026, under #StopRansomware.
  • Authors include CISA, FBI, NSA and South Korea’s National Police Agency.
  • Gunra emerged April 2025 from leaked Conti source; full RaaS by early 2026.
  • Initial access: Fortinet CVE-2024-55591 and CVE-2025-24472, plus exposed RDP/VPN/SSH.
  • Windows locker uses .ENCRT; Linux variant uses .GNRA — Linux files may be recoverable if timestamps survive.
  • Affiliates advertised an 80% cut; alias Golden Community used in recruitment.
